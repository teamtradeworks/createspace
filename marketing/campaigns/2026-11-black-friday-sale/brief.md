## Test: rand saving vs percentage

Every sale concept except the range ad has two twins, identical except for how the saving reads:

| Concept | Rand version | Percent version | What changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Robot Master "200+ builds" | `*-robot-master` | `*-robot-master-percent` | Headline line 3: "R 480 off." vs "20% off." |
| Superbot "26+ builds" | `*-superbot` | `*-superbot-percent` | "R 300 off." vs "20% off." |
| VinciBot "8 sensors" | `*-vincibot` | `*-vincibot-percent` | "R 520 off." vs "20% off." |
| Build it, code it, drive it | `*-build-code-drive` | `*-build-code-drive-percent` | Sticker: "R 480 off" vs "20% off" |
| Real engineering, disguised as play | `*-engineering-play` | `*-engineering-play-percent` | Sticker: "R 480 off" vs "20% off" |

Everything else matches: price line (struck was-price, red now-price), pill, CTA, landing page. The range ad stays percent-only ("Up to 20%") because its four products carry different savings.

**Hypothesis.** Above roughly R 100, a rand saving looks bigger than the percentage, so the rand twins should win on cost per purchase. All five products here are above R 1,000.

**How to run it.**
- Use Meta's A/B test (Experiments) with creative as the variable, rand vs percent, so Meta splits the audience evenly instead of the ad set favouring whichever twin starts faster. Put the five rand ads in arm A and the five percent ads in arm B.
- Keep the ad copy matched to the image: the percent twins use the percent copy below, the rand twins the rand copy.
- Primary metric: cost per purchase. With roughly a week and this account's volume (the best ad managed 67 purchases in 90 days), purchases alone may not reach significance, so read add-to-cart rate and link CTR alongside it, and compare the two arms in total rather than pair by pair.
- `utm_content` ends in `-percent` for every percent twin, so PostHog can split the two arms too.
