## Learning Outcomes (Expanded)

### Why Hands-On Learning Works

Hands-on, active learning creates measurably stronger outcomes than passive observation. Research from Natural Start Alliance highlights:

- A study with 105 first graders found that only **4% of children who performed hands-on tasks showed confusion** about a physics concept, compared to **~30% of passive observers** who failed to grasp the same concept.
- College students who physically manipulated objects outperformed peers who only watched demonstrations on subsequent quizzes about physics principles.
- Active physical engagement encodes both language and motor systems in the brain — creating "extra meaning" that makes concepts more intuitive and memorable.
- Children engaging in extensive hands-on exploration learn more words, particularly terms for "actions, forces, and physical objects."

**Source:** https://naturalstart.org/feature-stories/learning-doing-how-outdoor-play-prepares-kids-achievement-stem

When children launch a stomp rocket, they don't just read about air pressure — they *feel* it. They don't just hear about trajectory — they *observe* it change as they shift the launch angle. This direct physical experience encodes scientific concepts far more effectively than a textbook explanation.

### STEM Skills

**Physics & Aerodynamics:**
- Air pressure and pneumatic force (compressed air propelling the rocket)
- Newton's Third Law of Motion — every action has an equal and opposite reaction (stomping force → rocket thrust)
- Newton's First Law — a rocket at rest stays at rest until a force acts on it
- Trajectory and flight path — the arc a projectile follows under gravity
- Aerodynamic principles — how rocket shape (nose cone, foam tip) affects flight stability and drag
- Four forces of flight: thrust, drag, lift, and gravity
- Energy transfer — from kinetic energy of the stomp to potential/kinetic energy of the rocket in flight

**Scientific Inquiry:**
- Hypothesis formation — "What happens if I stomp harder / change the angle?"
- Observation and measurement — tracking flight height and distance
- Variable testing — adjusting angle, stomp force, and observing outcomes
- Cause and effect reasoning

**Mathematics:**
- Estimating distances and heights
- Understanding angle and trajectory (45° maximises range)
- Counting, comparing, and predicting

**Engineering Principles:**
- Understanding how launch angle affects range vs. height
- Optimising performance by adjusting variables (launch angle, force)
- Basic principles of rocket design (why foam-tipped nose cones reduce drag)

**Resilience & Growth Mindset:**
- Rockets don't always fly perfectly — children learn to troubleshoot (was the stomp firm enough? Was the angle right?) and try again
- The trial-and-error nature of adjusting launch parameters builds persistence and a "test it and see" mindset
- Research on outdoor STEM play shows children develop resilience when they "test, fail, and try again"

**Confidence & Self-Efficacy:**
- Successfully launching a rocket 100 feet into the air delivers an immediate, visible win that builds confidence
- Each successful launch gives children tangible proof of their own ability to make something happen through understanding and effort
- Children develop spatial confidence and physical confidence simultaneously

**Curiosity & Scientific Thinking:**
- The "why does that happen?" questions — why did the rocket go higher with a harder stomp? — build scientific curiosity habits
- Children spontaneously generate hypotheses and test them without being asked

**Physical Confidence:**
- The stomp launch requires coordination and whole-body movement, building gross motor skills
- Outdoor physical activity improves focus, concentration, and classroom readiness

**Teamwork & Communication:**
- Group play involves taking turns, sharing the launch pad, discussing predictions, and celebrating each other's launches
