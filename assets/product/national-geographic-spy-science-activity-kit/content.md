## Learning Outcomes (Expanded)

### Why Hands-On Learning Works

Research consistently shows that hands-on learning outperforms passive instruction for children. A University of Nebraska-Lincoln study found that students who engaged in hands-on activities retained **50% more information** than peers learning through traditional lectures. Experiential learning activates multiple brain regions simultaneously — when children move, touch, and experiment, they strengthen neural connections and improve long-term recall.

The National Science Teaching Association emphasises that experiential STEM learning makes content "real," connecting study to authentic applications. For children aged 8–12, spy-themed learning packages scientific inquiry inside a narrative that feels like adventure rather than instruction — dramatically increasing engagement and intrinsic motivation.

David Kolb's Experiential Learning Model (concrete experience → reflective observation → abstract conceptualisation → active experimentation) is the theoretical backbone behind activity kits like this: each mission cycles through all four stages naturally.

### STEM Skills

**Light & Physical Science:**
- How UV (ultraviolet) light differs from visible light (shorter wavelength, higher energy)
- Fluorescence: how certain substances absorb UV light and re-emit it as visible light
- How a periscope uses mirrors to redirect light — the physics of reflection
- How rearview spy glasses use angled mirrors to see behind

**Chemistry & Materials:**
- Properties of UV-reactive (fluorescent) inks
- Invisible ink chemistry: why UV-reactive substances like riboflavin (Vitamin B2) or quinine remain invisible under normal light

**Cryptography & Mathematics:**
- How substitution ciphers work (decoder wheel = Caesar cipher mechanism)
- Pattern recognition and frequency analysis in code-breaking
- Creating original cipher keys using the blank decoder wheel
- Logical reasoning applied to decoding messages

**Scientific Process:**
- Systematic observation and evidence collection
- Hypothesis-forming and deduction
- Note-taking, classifying, and organising information
- Intel analysis and logical conclusions from clues
