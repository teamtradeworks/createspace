## Learning Outcomes (Expanded)

### Why Hands-On Learning Works

Dig kits turn a passive topic (looking at pictures of rocks or planets) into an active investigation: children chip, brush, uncover, observe under a magnifier and then identify what they found against the poster. This "find it, look closely, name it" loop is the same observe-and-classify process real geologists use.

Penn State science education researcher Julia Plummer's *My Sky Tonight* project, which works with children aged 3 to 5, found that preschoolers can investigate, ask questions and make their own observations when given suitable materials, and with adult support begin forming claims from what they observe. Plummer: "They're coming to learn science for themselves and not something that's being told to them."

NSTA's *Science and Children* (2022) argues that space science suits young learners when it follows their interests and focuses on what they can see and handle, ideally integrating maths, art and literacy. A physical, planet-shaped object a child can hold and dig into is exactly that kind of concrete entry point to an otherwise abstract subject.

A 2025 scoping review in *Frontiers in Psychology* reports that kindergarten children spend on average 46% (range 36 to 66%) of class time on fine motor tasks such as writing, cutting and manipulating objects, and that 10 to 24% of children show delays in this area. Controlled tool use, like chipping and brushing with a small dig tool, exercises the same hand strength and precision.

### STEM Skills

**Earth Science & Geology:**
- Rocks and minerals are different things: granite and obsidian are rocks; pyrite, fluorite and quartz varieties are minerals
- Rock types: igneous rocks such as granite (cooled slowly underground) and obsidian (lava cooled too quickly to form crystals)
- Crystals and how they form (geode, amethyst)
- Identifying specimens by colour, lustre (metallic pyrite vs. glassy obsidian), banding (tiger's eye, chevron amethyst) and texture
- Vocabulary: specimen, crystal, mineral, gemstone, excavate, geologist

**Space Science & Astronomy:**
- The eight planets of the solar system and their order from the Sun
- Planet appearance: colours, bands, rings and surface features shown on the bricks and poster
- Rocky planets vs. gas giants (from the poster's solar system facts)
- Connecting rocks on Earth to rocks elsewhere in space (rocky planets, the Moon and meteorites are made of many of the same minerals)

**Scientific Observation & Classification:**
- Using a magnifying glass to see detail not visible to the naked eye
- Comparing and sorting specimens by colour, shape, shine and weight
- Matching finds to reference images on the poster (identification)
- Counting and checking (2 specimens per planet, 16 total), an early maths link

**Excavation Process:**
- Working methodically and carefully so specimens are not damaged
- Using water to soften material: observing that a dry, hard brick becomes easier to dig when wet (cause and effect)

- Deciding when to add water and how much

**Resilience & Growth Mindset:**

- Eight bricks give eight chances to get better at technique

**Emotional Regulation:**

- Pacing: stopping and resuming across several sessions

**Confidence & Self-Esteem:**

- Being able to name real gemstones and planets and explain them to others

**Creativity & Innovation:**
- Open-ended play afterwards: arranging planets, displaying the collection, making up space-mining stories

**Social Skills & Sharing:**
- Two dig tools in the box: siblings, friends or a parent and child can dig side by side
- Taking turns, sharing finds and comparing specimens

**Fine Motor Skills:**
- Controlled chipping and brushing builds hand strength, grip and hand-eye coordination used in writing and cutting

### Future-Ready Skills & Career Connections

- **Geology and earth science:** geologists identify rocks and minerals for mining, construction, water resources and environmental work. South Africa is one of the world's major mining economies (gold, platinum, diamonds, iron ore, coal), so geology is a locally relevant career path.
- **Planetary science and space exploration:** planetary geologists study rocks on the Moon and Mars, analyse meteorites, and help choose landing sites for rovers. South Africa hosts major astronomy facilities (SALT, MeerKAT, the SKA project).
- **Gemology and materials science:** identifying, grading and working with gemstones and minerals.
- **Transferable skills:** careful observation, classification and patience underpin careers in medicine, laboratory science, archaeology and engineering.

- Specimens are genuine rocks and gemstones (not plastic)
