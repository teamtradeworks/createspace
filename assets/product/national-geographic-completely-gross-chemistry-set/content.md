## Learning Outcomes (Expanded)

### Why Hands-On Learning Works
**Sources:** https://www.getsuperspace.com/blogs/play-magazine/hands-on-learning-benefits ; https://www.structural-learning.com/post/hands-on-learning ; https://iojpe.org/index.php/iojpe/article/download/111/114

Research consistently shows that hands-on, active learning dramatically outperforms passive instruction:
- Learners remember up to 75% of what they practice by doing, compared to just 5% from lectures (Edgar Dale's cone of experience)
- A study measuring effectiveness of hands-on science activities found average test scores increased from 58.2 to 83.2 (a post-test improvement of 25 points, or 43%)
- Students show higher levels of participation, intrinsic motivation, and confidence when learning through experiments
- When science is simplified, contextualised, and linked to practical experiences, students become more motivated

The gross-theme approach adds an additional engagement layer: the emotional response to something disgusting is deeply memorable, making the associated science concepts stick far more effectively than a textbook exercise.

### STEM Skills

**Chemistry — Polymer Science:**
- Understanding how molecules cross-link to form polymers (slime, brain mold)
- How chain length and structure affect material properties (why slime behaves the way it does)
- Reversible vs irreversible reactions

**Chemistry — Acid-Base Reactions:**
- pH concepts and indicators (phenol red changes colour in response to pH)
- Acid and base identification
- Reaction products and gas production (carbon dioxide bubbles)

**Chemistry — Phosphorescence & Fluorescence:**
- Difference between fluorescence and phosphorescence
- How zinc sulfide absorbs and emits light
- Glow-in-the-dark science principles

**Chemistry — Non-Newtonian Fluids:**
- Materials that don't follow standard liquid/solid behaviour
- Shear-thickening and shear-thinning fluids
- Real-world applications (oobleck, quicksand, body armour)

**Biology — Anatomy:**
- Structure and function of body parts (brain, eyes, gut)
- Why bodily fluids have the properties they do
- Scientific terms for gross body processes

**Scientific Process:**
- Measuring, mixing, observing, and recording results
- Forming hypotheses before each experiment
- Comparing expected vs actual outcomes
- Understanding cause and effect

### Life Skills & Character Development

**Sources:** https://www.crafts4kids.co.uk/blogs/news/sparking-young-minds-the-power-of-stem-kits ; https://wonderlab.co.in/blogs/news/how-science-kits-help-children-develop-critical-thinking-and-problem-solving

**Critical Thinking & Problem-Solving:**
- Every experiment asks children to predict what will happen before they do it (hypothesis formation)
- Comparing expected results to actual outcomes builds analytical thinking
- When something doesn't go as planned, kids must troubleshoot and adjust — mirroring the real scientific process

**Resilience & Growth Mindset:**
- Experiments that don't go as expected teach children that "failure" is data, not defeat
- Repeating an experiment with modified inputs builds persistence
- The iterative nature of scientific inquiry develops a growth mindset: effort and adjustment lead to success

**Confidence & Self-Esteem:**
- Successfully completing experiments gives children tangible proof of their capability
- The "wow" moments from gross results create memorable positive associations with science
- Children who succeed at chemistry experiments begin to see themselves as "scientists" — an identity shift that supports future STEM engagement

**Curiosity & Intrinsic Motivation:**
- The gross-theme premise hooks children emotionally, converting reluctant learners into enthusiastic participants
- Following up on "why did that happen?" introduces a lifetime habit of scientific curiosity

**Fine Motor Skills:**
- Measuring small amounts, using pipettes, pouring precisely, and moulding materials all develop fine motor control
- Accuracy requirements teach careful, methodical working habits
