**Button functions (from Activity Guide):**
| Button | Colour/shape | Function |
|---|---|---|
| FORWARD | Blue arrow (top) | Moves forward one step (5" / 12.5 cm) |
| REVERSE | Yellow arrow (bottom) | Moves backward one step |
| ROTATE RIGHT | Purple arrow | Turns 90° right and stops |
| ROTATE LEFT | Orange arrow | Turns 90° left and stops |
| ACTION | Red oval (behind left ear) | One of 3 random actions: move forward and back; loud "SQUEAAKK"; CHIRP-CHIRP-CHIRP with light-up eyes |
| GO | Round green (centre) | Runs the programmed steps, up to 40 |
| CLEAR | Yellow oval (behind right ear) | Press and hold to clear memory; confirmation tone |

**Coding cards:** Colour-coordinated to match the buttons. Double-sided: the front shows the directional arrow command, the back shows the position of the mouse. A red "Lightning Bolt" card represents the ACTION command. Kids line the cards up in order to mirror each step of the program (e.g. FORWARD, FORWARD, TURN RIGHT, FORWARD, ACTION).

## Learning Outcomes (Expanded)

### Why Hands-On Learning Works

- **Early childhood robotics and computational thinking (meta-analysis):** Alonso-García et al. (2024, *Heliyon*) reviewed robotics interventions in early childhood education and concluded they have a significant effect on children's computational thinking. They also found that contexts that prioritise these skills matter more than access to technology.
- **Robotics and cognitive outcomes in primary school:** Mukhasheva et al. (2023, *European Journal of Educational Research*) pooled 8 studies (567 children) and found a medium effect size (SMD = 0.641) in favour of robotics activities, with a slightly larger effect for Grades 1 to 3 than Grades 4 to 6.
- **K-12 robotics and computational thinking:** A 2021 systematic review/meta-analysis in the *Journal of Educational Computing Research* (17 studies) found a medium overall effect (SMD = 0.46), with computational thinking gains of SMD = 0.48.
- **Floor robots (Bee-Bot, same "grid-agent" format as Colby):** A study of 47 preschoolers in Crete (2019-2020) found statistically significant computational thinking gains after a floor-robot programme versus a control group. A Salamanca, Spain study (40 early-years children, pre/post-test with control group) found significantly better action-sequencing in children who worked with the robots.
- **Grid-based robots and maths:** Design-based research in kindergarten links forward/backward/rotate grid robots to spatial reasoning and measurement, as well as algorithmic thinking and debugging.

Key insight: young children learn coding concepts best when the "program" is physical. They can hold the cards, count the squares and watch a real object carry out (or fail to carry out) their instructions.

### STEM Skills

**Coding Foundations:**
- Sequencing: putting instructions in the right order
- Algorithms: planning a step-by-step route before running it
- Debugging: spotting where the code went wrong and fixing it
- Program memory/clearing: understanding that a program must be reset before a new one
- If-then logic (listed by manufacturer)
- Commands and execution (programming vs pressing GO)

**Maths & Spatial Reasoning:**
- Counting spaces/steps between mouse and cheese
- Calculating distance (each step = one square)
- Directional language: forward, backward, left, right
- 90° turns and orientation (which way the nose is pointing)
- Spatial concepts and mapping a route on a grid
- Efficiency: finding the fewest steps

**Engineering & Design:**
- Building maze structures from walls and tunnels
- Designing original mazes and challenges for others

Manufacturer list, "What can a programmable robot teach?": Problem solving; Self-correcting errors; Critical thinking; Analytical thinking; If-then logic; Working collaboratively with others; Discussion and communication skills; Calculating distance; Spatial concepts.

**Open-ended activities (from guide and reviews):**
- **Meet the mouse:** press one arrow then GO to explore each button in isolation
- **Free-roam coding:** Hyper speed on the floor; build mazes from books, blocks or household items
- **Design your own maze:** build a custom maze, then program Colby from start to finish
- **Challenge a friend:** build a maze for a sibling, friend or parent to solve
- **Make it harder (guide's suggested progression):** add 1 to 2 more spaces between mouse and cheese, add more walls, add a turn before the cheese
- **Multiplayer race:** with a second mouse (Jack, LER2841, sold separately)
- **At-Home Activities PDF** (linked from Stemfinity) offers further ideas

**Difficulty Progression:** Cards are numbered 1 to 20 and explicitly ramp up: "Start out with Card 1 to learn the very basics, and follow in number sequence as your logic and critical thinking skills improve." Progression goes: straight lines → first turn → reversed orientation → irregular board shapes → ACTION command → tunnels as waypoints → combined walls, tunnels and ACTION.

**Guide's beginner sequence (Tips for Beginning Programmers):**
1. Identify the colour and function of each button; reinforce that green means GO
2. Press the blue arrow once then GO; notice Colby moves the way his nose points
3. Explore the turn arrows one at a time (90° rotation in place)
4. Always press and hold CLEAR before a new program, so children see each movement in isolation
5. Build a 4 x 4 grid, set up Card 1, count the spaces, find the matching coding cards (two forward), then program
6. "Begin with short series of moves, before gradually adding in turns and building different maze configurations. Most of all, keep it fun!"

## Additional Notes

### Quality & Safety
- Durable plastic, bright colours (reviewer)
- Warning: "CHOKING HAZARD - Small parts. Not for children under 3 years."
- Do not push the mouse forwards or backwards by hand, as this can damage the wheels and axles
