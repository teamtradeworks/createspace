## Learning Outcomes (Expanded)

This is a physical active-play product for 3 to 6 year olds, not a coding or electronics kit. Learning outcomes centre on motor development, body awareness, colour recognition and imaginative play.

### Why Active, Movement-Based Play Works
- **WHO guidelines (2019)** for children under 5: children aged 1 to 2 should spend at least 180 minutes a day in a variety of physical activities; for ages 3 to 4, those 180 minutes should include at least 60 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous activity. WHO frames this as active play, not structured exercise, and recommends no more than 1 hour of sedentary screen time a day for 2 to 4 year olds.
- **Balance is foundational:** University of Nevada Extension material describes balance as "an essential prerequisite of almost all movement skills". Preschool skill frameworks expect children to stand on one foot for about 5 seconds before starting school.
- **Sensory systems:** NDNA (UK National Day Nurseries Association) activity guidance explains that balancing work develops the vestibular sense (head position and movement) and proprioception (sensing where the body is in space), which "helps children to gain coordination and balance". Its suggested progression moves from flat walkways to stones or blocks at different heights for stepping up and down.
- **Occupational therapy use:** Paediatric therapists (The Inspired Treehouse) use stepping stones for balance, coordination, proprioception, motor planning, gross motor skills and following directions. Single-leg stance and dynamic balance are needed for PE, playground play and climbing stairs safely.
- **Motor skills and cognition (UK Millennium Cohort Study, Zhou & Tolmie, Brain Sciences 2024, n = 3,188):** gross motor skills in infancy were associated with spatial working memory and PE performance at age 11. Effects were small; present as an association, not a cause.
- **Executive function link:** A 2019 review in Early Childhood Research Quarterly argues executive function and motor skills co-develop in young children. Kindergarten studies (Cameron et al.; ECLS-K analyses) find early motor skills and executive function both predict later maths and reading growth. Evidence is mostly observational.
- **Confidence and self-esteem:** Physical literacy research treats confidence and motivation as core parts of movement development. Children proficient in fundamental movement skills are reported to have better self-esteem, social skills and more positive attitudes to physical activity (correlational). Children who do not master these skills are more likely to drop out of physical activity later in life.
- **Preschoolers' own view:** A 2025 study of preschool children's perspectives on physical literacy found that their motivation to move is tied to how movement makes them feel; the enjoyment/affective side is central to engagement.

Caveat: no peer-reviewed research was found on stepping stones specifically. Benefits should be framed as what balance and active play support, not as proven outcomes of this product.

### STEM Skills

**Body Science & Physical Development:**
- Static balance (standing on one stone, on one foot)
- Dynamic balance (moving stone to stone)
- Gross motor skills: stepping, hopping, jumping, landing
- Coordination and bilateral coordination
- Proprioception and vestibular development (body awareness)
- Motor planning (working out how to get from one stone to the next)

**Spatial Reasoning:**
- Judging distance and gaps between stones
- Designing paths and layouts (curves, lines, circles, spaced-out courses)
- Understanding position words (on, off, next to, across, between)

**Early Maths & Cognition:**
- Colour recognition and matching (spinner to stone)
- Counting stones and steps
- Patterns and sequences (e.g. red, blue, red, blue paths)
- Following multi-step directions and game rules

The kit has **one included game** (the colour spinner game). Everything else is open-ended. The ideas below come from the manufacturer copy, occupational therapy activity guides and common stepping-stone play; they are **not** a list printed in the box.

**Summary:**
- **Projects:** 0 (no guided builds)
- **Challenges:** 1 included (spinner game) + suggested open-ended challenges below
- **Activities:** suggested play ideas below (not included as a printed list)

| # | Name | Type | Description | Skills/Concepts Taught |
|---|------|------|-------------|----------------------|
| 1 | Spinner colour jump (included) | Challenge | Lay out the stones, spin the dial, and jump to the stone matching the colour it lands on | Colour recognition, balance, jumping, following rules, turn-taking |
| 2 | Stone-to-stone walk | Activity | Walk across stones laid in a line or circle without stepping off | Dynamic balance, coordination |
| 3 | Obstacle course | Challenge | Build a course with stones plus pillows, tables to crawl under and furniture to weave around | Motor planning, spatial reasoning, creativity |

| 5 | Bend and reach | Activity | Walk the path and squat to pick up a named toy next to a stone | Balance while bending, listening, following directions |
| 6 | Step downs | Activity | Stand on one stone and tap the other foot to the floor or a marker beside it, then step back up | Single-leg balance, leg strength |
| 7 | Bear crawl crossing | Activity | Cross the stones on hands and feet without touching the floor | Upper-body strength, coordination |
| 8 | Balance and catch | Activity | Stand on one or two stones and toss a ball or beanbag back and forth | Balance under distraction, hand-eye coordination |
| 9 | One-foot contest | Challenge | Who can stand on one foot on a stone the longest? | Static balance, friendly competition |
| 10 | Freeze path | Activity | Move along the stones to music and freeze when it stops | Self-control, listening, balance |
| 11 | Colour patterns and counting | Activity | Make colour patterns with the stones and count steps across | Patterns, counting, colour names |
| 12 | Make it harder | Challenge | Space stones further apart, curve the path, add objects to step over, go barefoot | Progressive challenge, motor planning, proprioception |
| 13 | Small-world play | Activity | Use stones as islands, ponds or animal pens for toy animals | Imaginative play, storytelling |

**Difficulty Progression:** Not built in. Difficulty is set by the layout: stones placed close together (both feet side by side) are easiest; wider gaps, curves, single-foot challenges and spinner games are harder. This lets the set grow with the child from age 3 to 6.
