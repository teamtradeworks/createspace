### Manufacturer Information

**Source:** https://microbit.org/buy/bbc-microbit-club/

The BBC micro:bit Club is the official classroom/club pack from the Micro:bit Educational Foundation. It bundles 10 complete Go kits (each containing a micro:bit V2 board + accessories) into a single economical box — the definitive solution for getting an entire group coding at the same time.

### Product Overview

The BBC micro:bit Club pack contains 10 complete micro:bit V2 Go bundles. Each bundle is a fully self-contained coding starter kit, giving every learner in the group their own board, power supply, and getting-started materials. This eliminates the need for schools, clubs, or makerspaces to source individual boards separately.

The micro:bit V2 is a pocket-sized computer measuring just 4 cm × 5 cm. Despite its small size, it is a capable single-board computer with an ARM Cortex-M4 processor, 512 KB Flash, 128 KB RAM, built-in speaker and microphone, a full suite of sensors, and Bluetooth/radio wireless communication. It can be coded with drag-and-drop blocks in Microsoft MakeCode, text-based Python (MicroPython), JavaScript, or Scratch.

The device is actively used in over 60 countries and has reached over 20 million young people worldwide (Micro:bit Educational Foundation, 2024).

## Learning Outcomes (Expanded)

### Why Hands-On Learning Works

Research consistently shows that children learn more effectively through hands-on, physical engagement with material than through passive instruction. The BBC micro:bit bridges this gap by making abstract coding concepts tangible — students see immediate, real-world results from their code.

**Key research findings:**

- 80% of students taught with micro:bit agreed that "computing and technology is easier to understand than other subjects", compared to just 52% of those who had not used micro:bit (Micro:bit Educational Foundation consolidated research, 2024)
- 80% of girls agreed that coding is a useful skill to learn after learning with micro:bit — directly addressing the technology gender gap
- 84% or more teachers agreed that using micro:bit resources increased their confidence in teaching computing (2024 consolidated research)
- 90% or more teachers said they were confident or very confident to use their learning and materials with students
- A 2024 study on elementary STEM teachers found that micro:bit programming training activities effectively enhanced computational thinking and programming attitudes
- Research with middle school students found improved algorithmic thinking, problem-solving skills, and increased interest in programming after micro:bit use

The physical, tangible nature of the device is a key learning accelerator: when a child shakes the board and sees the dice number change, or claps near the microphone and watches an LED light up, abstract concepts like "input", "output", "variables", and "conditionals" become immediately concrete and understandable.

### STEM Skills

**Computing & Programming Fundamentals:**
- Sequences (ordering instructions to produce a result)
- Selection / conditionals (if/else logic)
- Loops and iteration (repeat, while, for)
- Variables (storing and updating data)
- Functions and subroutines (reusable blocks of code)
- Event-driven programming (respond to button presses, gestures, sounds)
- Debugging (finding and fixing errors in code)
- Algorithms (designing step-by-step solutions)

**Electronics & Physical Computing:**
- Inputs and outputs (sensing the world and responding to it)
- Sensors: how accelerometers, microphones, light sensors, and temperature sensors work
- Wireless communication: radio and Bluetooth protocols
- Edge connector: how to interface with external components (no soldering required)
- Power management and basic electronics safety

**Data & Science:**
- Data logging (recording readings over time)
- Sensor calibration and measurement accuracy
- Basic data analysis (plotting and interpreting readings)
- Scientific method (forming hypotheses, testing, drawing conclusions)

**Mathematics:**
- Coordinate systems (LED display grid)
- Binary logic (LED on/off, boolean variables)
- Probability and random numbers (dice, coin flip games)
- Measurement units (temperature °C, acceleration m/s²)

**Computational Thinking:**
- Decomposition (breaking big problems into smaller steps)
- Pattern recognition (identifying repeating structures)
- Abstraction (hiding complexity behind simple controls)
- Algorithm design (planning solutions before coding)

### Life Skills & Character Development

**Critical Thinking & Problem-Solving:**
- Every coding project requires learners to plan, attempt, observe the result, and adjust — a core problem-solving loop that transfers to academic and life challenges
- Debugging (finding why code doesn't work as expected) teaches systematic thinking and persistence
- Designing solutions to open-ended challenges develops lateral thinking

**Resilience & Growth Mindset:**
- The iterative nature of coding — try, fail, adjust, retry — directly practises growth mindset ("I can't do this yet")
- Research specifically notes that micro:bit lessons cultivate resilience and that teachers observed all learners achieving a sense of success, particularly those with additional needs (Springer, 2025)
- The immediate visual/audio feedback from the board makes success feel tangible and frequent, reinforcing motivation

**Collaboration & Communication:**
- The Club pack's 10-board setup enables genuine pair and group programming activities
- Multiplayer radio games require turn-taking, communication, and teamwork
- Group science experiments with shared data logging build collaborative skills

**Creativity & Self-Expression:**
- Projects span music, art, fashion, and storytelling — not just "traditional" STEM
- Custom LED animations, personalised name badges, and original game designs give learners a creative voice through technology
- The micro:bit's versatility means there is always room to add "one more thing" to a project

**Confidence & Self-Efficacy:**
- Immediate feedback from the physical device creates frequent "it works!" moments
- Research shows that after using micro:bit, 80% of students (including 80% of girls) feel coding is a useful skill to learn — shifting self-belief
- Building something physical that works — a game, a musical instrument, a robot — creates visible, shareable proof of competence

## Additional Notes

### Quality & Safety

- Manufactured to meet EU and international safety standards; CE and FCC certifications
- RoHS compliant (no hazardous materials)
- Designed by the Micro:bit Educational Foundation (UK non-profit), a purpose-built organisation dedicated to STEM education
- No sharp edges, no small fragile parts (the board itself is robust and suitable for classroom handling)
- No soldering required — completely safe for primary and secondary school use
- Battery holders are standard AAA type — safe and easy to replace

### Longevity

- The micro:bit is not a "use once and forget" kit — the hardware is durable and reusable across many projects, years, and cohorts
- Free online project library is actively maintained and grows continuously
- Fully compatible with a vast ecosystem of expansion boards and accessories (robotics kits, maker kits, smart home sensors, etc.)
- New capabilities (AI/machine learning via CreateAI) are added to the platform over time, extending the device's useful life
- The 10-board Club format means the kit is used by group after group — cost per learner is very low over time
- Strong community: hackster.io alone has 243+ community projects; YouTube has thousands of tutorials

### Build Time

- Getting started (first LED animation, first button project): 15–30 minutes
- A structured beginner lesson (e.g. dice, step counter): 45–60 minutes
- More complex projects (radio game, science experiment): 2–4 hours
- Full multi-session projects (robot, wearable, data logger): 4–8+ hours across multiple sessions

1. **10 boards in one box** — the only micro:bit product designed specifically for simultaneous group learning; unlocks radio/multiplayer functionality not possible with a single board
2. **The official foundation product** — made by the Micro:bit Educational Foundation (a UK non-profit), not a third-party copy; ensures full software support, curriculum integration, and educational resources

4. **AI-ready** — CreateAI on the micro:bit platform introduces machine learning concepts, future-proofing the curriculum beyond basic coding
5. **Wireless out of the box** — Bluetooth 5.0 and 2.4 GHz radio built in, enabling multi-board experiments immediately
