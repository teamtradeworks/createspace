## Learning Outcomes (Expanded)

### Why Hands-On Learning Works
- **Fine motor skills predict later school success.** Grissmer et al. (2010) analysed six large longitudinal datasets and found fine motor skills at school entry were a strong predictor of later achievement, about as strong as attention for maths. Cameron and colleagues (University of Virginia) report that executive function, fine motor skills and general knowledge in kindergarten predict Grade 8 reading and maths better than early literacy skills. Grissmer's team proposes that brain networks used for motor learning are later reused for maths concepts.
- **Fine motor integration and early maths.** A UK study (Frontiers in Psychology, 2016) found fine motor integration predicted early maths ability even after controlling for nonverbal IQ, and recommended that fine motor skills play a central role in early maths interventions. (Not every study agrees: Malone et al., 2022, found domain-specific skills rather than fine motor skills predicted later outcomes, so this is a supporting rather than settled finding.)
- **Construction play builds spatial and maths thinking.** Verdine et al. (2014, Child Development) tested 100+ three-year-olds: children better at copying block structures also did better in early maths. Follow-up work with 4 and 5-year-olds linked building behaviours and structural complexity to current and later spatial skills and to maths skills. Spatial skills support complex maths problem-solving in later school years.
- **Early engineering is developmentally appropriate.** Bagiati and Evangelou (Purdue) developed and tested an early engineering curriculum for 3 to 5-year-olds, and studied how preschoolers explore familiar and unfamiliar artifacts to encourage engineering thinking. Their work frames tinkering, building and exploring how objects work as natural engineering behaviours at this age.

### STEM Skills

**Early Engineering & Construction:**
- How parts fit together and are fastened (snap joints vs bolted joints)
- Assembling and disassembling a structure; cause and effect (turning the screwdriver tightens or loosens a bolt)
- Using a real-world tool correctly (a screwdriver) and understanding what tools are for
- Introduces the idea of a robot and moving parts (swivel joints)

**Maths & Spatial Thinking:**
- Colour matching and sorting bolts (manufacturer: "matching, patterning")
- Creating colour patterns with the bolts
- Counting bolts in and out
- Spatial reasoning: lining a bolt up with a hole, orientation, position words (top, side, on, in)

**Fine Motor & Physical Development:**
- Hand-eye coordination (lining up screwdriver and bolt)
- Bilateral coordination: one hand stabilises the robot while the other turns the screwdriver
- Grip, wrist rotation and hand strength from twisting
- Pincer grasp from picking up bolts and peeling/placing stickers

### Future-Ready Skills & Career Connections
- Fine motor control, tool handling and spatial reasoning underpin hands-on technical work: engineering, mechanics, robotics, electronics, manufacturing and the skilled trades.
- **South African context:** The 2025 Xpatweb Critical Skills Survey (381 employers) found 38% struggled to hire engineers, up from about 23% in 2024 and 14% in 2018. Difficulty hiring artisans doubled from 10% to 22%, with electricians, millwrights, automation specialists and instrumentation specialists hardest to find. ICT specialist shortages rose to 22%.
- **Girls and construction play:** A UNESCO/IEA analysis of TIMSS 2019 data found 67% of parents often play with building blocks or construction toys with boys, but only 58% with girls. Virginia Tech research on construction sets found girls report less fun and perceive assembly as harder by age 9, and blame themselves for difficulties. Early, positive tool-and-building experiences for all children are one response.
