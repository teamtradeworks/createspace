## Learning Outcomes (Expanded)

### Why Hands-On Learning Works

Building a 3D model from flat, interlocking parts is a spatial-reasoning exercise: children must look at 2D pieces and illustrated steps and work out how they form a 3D animal.

- A study of 729 children aged 7–12 linked 3D mental rotation ability to arithmetic performance, suggesting spatial enrichment activities can support maths (Frontiers in Psychology, 2019).
- A study of 168 middle-schoolers found that progress in a 3D puzzle task tracked with scores on the Purdue Spatial Visualization Test (Frontiers in Education, 2019). Correlational, not causal.
- A 2024 literature review concluded spatial reasoning is malleable in children and puzzle play is among the most effective ways to build it; spatial skill quality is a good predictor of later success in STEM disciplines.
- A 2022 meta-analysis found spatial training produced an average 0.28 standard deviation improvement in mathematics outcomes, with larger effects when physical manipulatives were used (cited in sibling marble-run research).
- Hobby/modelling sources describe model building as strong practice for fine motor control, patience and instruction-following. These are not peer-reviewed; treat as general claims.

Caveat: evidence that 3D puzzles specifically raise STEM grades is mostly correlational or indirect.

### STEM Skills

**Spatial Reasoning & Geometry:**
- Translating flat (2D) pieces into a 3D form
- Mental rotation: orienting pieces to match illustrated steps
- Understanding cross-sections and ribs/frame construction (laser-cut slot-together models build a body from interlocking profiles)
- Symmetry (left and right flippers, tail flukes)

**Engineering & Mechanisms:**
- How a motor converts electrical energy into movement
- How a motorised base produces a repeating "swimming" motion (simple kinetic mechanism)
- Mounting and balance: attaching the model so it moves smoothly
- Interlocking/friction-fit joints instead of glue or screws

**Electricity & Light:**
- USB power as an energy source
- LED colour modes, colour mixing (colour-cycle mode) and dimming

**Life Science / Marine Biology (via Learning Guide and the model):**
- Whale anatomy: flukes, pectoral flippers, blowhole, throat grooves (humpback)
- How whales swim: flukes move up and down (unlike fish tails, which move side to side), because whales evolved from land mammals whose spines flex vertically; long muscles above and below the backbone power the strokes; flippers steer
- Whales as mammals (breathe air, warm-blooded, feed young milk)
- Migration and ocean conservation (National Geographic mission: "inspire people to care about the planet")

**South African context (useful local hook):**
- Southern right whales migrate from Antarctic feeding grounds to South African waters roughly June to December; Hermanus (Walker Bay) is often called one of the best land-based whale-watching spots in the world, with whales sometimes as close as 5 m from shore.
- Humpback whales pass along the coast (roughly May to December), moving up from the Western Cape to KwaZulu-Natal.
- Bryde's whales are year-round residents.
