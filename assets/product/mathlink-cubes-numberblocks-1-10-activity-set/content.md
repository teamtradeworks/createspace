## Learning Outcomes (Expanded)

### Why Hands-On Learning Works
- **Concrete manipulatives help maths stick.** Carbonneau, Marley & Selig (2013) meta-analysis in *Journal of Educational Psychology* (55 studies, 7,237 learners) found statistically significant small-to-moderate effects in favour of teaching maths with manipulatives over abstract symbols only, with moderate-to-large effects on retention. Effects depended on how the manipulatives were used, with guided, explicit instruction working best (the activity guide provides exactly this structure). Secondary reviews note effects were weaker for 3-6 year olds overall, so the guided episode-linked structure matters.
- **Early maths is the strongest predictor of later school success.** Duncan et al. (2007), "School readiness and later achievement" (*Developmental Psychology*, six longitudinal datasets): mastery of early maths concepts at school entry was the strongest predictor of later academic achievement, ahead of reading and attention. Early maths predicted later reading as well as later maths. (Note: some researchers, e.g. Clancy Blair, criticised the conclusions.)
- **Subitising and part-whole thinking are foundations of number sense.** Subitising (recognising small quantities instantly without counting, typically up to 5) and understanding that numbers are made of smaller parts (part-part-whole) are core early number sense skills; England's EYFS expects children to subitise up to 5. Numberblocks makes these visible because every number is literally built from that many blocks.
- **Link to the show's design:** Numberblocks was developed with the NCETM (National Centre for Excellence in the Teaching of Mathematics), whose team worked closely on the series so it delivers essential numeracy skills that build good number sense, in line with the maths mastery approach. The NCETM published support materials for Series 1 and 2 for Reception and Year 1 teachers. Numberblocks won the 2019 BAFTA for Pre-School Animation (per NCETM news). The full series runs to 90 episodes, from number names and counting through addition, subtraction, multiplication and division.

### STEM Skills

**Number recognition & counting:**
- Counting to 10 and counting back from 10 (Blast Off)
- One-to-one correspondence (placing a cube on each object)
- Matching numerals (Numberlings) to quantities
- Zero as "nothing" and one less than one
- Ordinal language (first, second, third...)

**Number sense & structure:**
- Subitising numbers 1-5 (recognising amounts without counting)
- Conservation of number (same amount, different arrangement)
- Part-part-whole and partitioning
- Number bonds to 5 and to 10; bonds of 6, 7, 8 and 9
- Numbers 6-10 as "5 and a bit"
- Ten ones equal one ten (base-ten foundation)
- Odd and even numbers; equal groups

**Operations:**
- Addition and subtraction of 1, then of larger numbers
- Writing number sentences (e.g. 3 = 1 + 2)
- Missing-number problems (2 + ? = 5)
- Comparing numbers: greater than, less than, equal to
- Early multiplication as repeated addition (doubling, tripling)
- Early division as sharing into equal groups (factors of 6)

**Shape, space & pattern:**
- Square numbers (4 and 9) and properties of a square
- 2D shapes: counting sides and corners (Flatland)
- Repeating colour patterns (Pattern Palace)
- Spatial reasoning: rebuilding shapes from outlines

**Fine motor:**
- Pushing cubes together and twisting them apart; fitting faceplates; applying stickers

**Difficulty Progression:** Ordered by difficulty in two colour-coded levels. Red cards (1-11) cover numbers 0-5: counting, ordering, subitising, part-whole, +/- 1. Orange cards (12-30) cover 6-10 and introduce bonds to 10, odd/even, base ten, subtraction of larger numbers, shapes, patterns, missing numbers and early multiplication/division. Activities broadly follow the episode order of Series 1-3 (Red Level One episodes, then Orange Level Two episodes on learningblocks.tv). Most activities include an extension step.

### Life Skills & Character Development

**Critical Thinking & Problem-Solving:**
- Missing-number puzzles (Big Tum), detective reasoning (The Wrong Number), checking work by laying blocks over outlines
- Self-correcting design: if the blocks don't fit the picture, children see it straight away

**Resilience & Growth Mindset:**
- Many activities ask "can you find another way?", building the idea that problems have more than one solution
- Characters model persistence (Numberblock Rally: work back until One wins)

**Emotional Regulation:**
- Characters have distinct personalities and feelings (One feeling lonely, Three wanting to be tallest), opening conversations about emotions alongside maths
- Turn-taking and "challenge a friend" activities

**Confidence & Self-Esteem:**
- Familiar, loved TV characters lower the barrier to maths; children recognise friends rather than abstract numbers
- Early wins at the red level build confidence before the orange level

**Creativity & Innovation:**
- Making new shapes for each Numberblock, inventing patterns, acting out episodes, storytelling with characters

**Language & Communication:**
- Maths talk prompted throughout ("Five minus One is Four", "greater means bigger"); songs and rhymes from the episodes

**Fine Motor Skills:**
- Linking and twisting cubes, attaching faceplates, applying stickers, writing on wipe-clean cards
