## Learning Outcomes (Expanded)

### Why Hands-On Learning Works

Research consistently demonstrates that hands-on, screen-free coding with educational robots is more effective for developing computational thinking skills than unplugged coding activities in preschool settings. A study published in the British Journal of Educational Technology found that robot programming groups experienced greater gains in sequencing ability over time compared to block play control groups.

A landmark study on coding in primary grades found that improvements in planning and inhibition skills after just 1 month of coding activities were equivalent to or greater than the improvement attained after 7 months of standard educational activities — supporting the hypothesis that learning computational thinking via coding can significantly boost children's spontaneous development of executive functions. (Source: PMC/NIH study "Coding in Primary Grades Boosts Children's Executive Functions")

Research with the KIBO screen-free robot (a comparable product) demonstrated that the screen-free group performed 27% better than the screen-based group on tests of computational thinking skills including sequencing, understanding of loops and conditionals, and debugging. This validates the Tale-Bot Pro's screen-free approach.

Studies also show there are no gender-related differences in programming ability — girls feel as comfortable as boys about their ability to read, write, and analyze computer programs. Recent literature confirms girls and boys performed similarly in programming and computational thinking, making screen-free coding robots an excellent tool for inclusive STEM education.

**Key insight:** Screen-free programmable robotics can be a developmentally appropriate tool to start teaching programming as young as three years old. Children benefit from connecting programming concepts to concrete, physical objects, reinforcing learning in an age-appropriate way.

### Life Skills & Character Development

**Critical Thinking & Problem-Solving:**
- Children learn to break down problems into smaller, manageable steps
- Trial-and-error approach builds systematic problem-solving habits
- Debugging teaches children to identify errors and find solutions independently
- The PRIMM framework (Predict, Run, Investigate, Modify, Make) is supported — children predict outcomes, test, investigate issues, and modify their approach

**Resilience & Growth Mindset:**
- Hands-on STEM education develops a growth mindset because it allows children to see firsthand that the only limits that exist are the ones they place on themselves
- When code doesn't work as expected, children learn to persist, analyse, and try again
- The iterative nature of coding (plan → test → debug → retry) normalises making mistakes as part of learning
- Research shows coding activities improve self-regulation, particularly in children who start with lower self-regulation skills

**Emotional Regulation:**
- Research published in the British Journal of Educational Technology found that robot programming improved executive function skills in preschoolers, including working memory, cognitive flexibility, and inhibitory control
- The structured nature of coding (plan, execute, observe result) teaches patience and delayed gratification
- Managing frustration when the robot doesn't do what was expected builds frustration tolerance
- The early childhood period (ages 2-6) is when executive function skills rapidly transform — coding activities during this window are particularly impactful

**Confidence & Self-Esteem:**
- Immediate visual feedback (robot moves, draws, speaks) provides tangible evidence of achievement
- Progressive challenges allow children to build skills incrementally, experiencing success at each stage
- The screen-free, button-based interface ensures even very young children can succeed independently
- Hands-on, explorative approaches empower children's imaginations, build their confidence, and motivate them to take more interest in their own education
- STEM experiences help children overcome STEM anxiety and gain confidence in their abilities

**Creativity & Innovation:**
- Open-ended design empowers children to imagine adventures independently
- Storytelling features (voice recording, themed maps, blank maps) encourage narrative creation
- LEGO compatibility enables children to build and customise their robot
- Paper craft items and stickers add creative expression beyond coding
- Cross-curricular integration (science + coding + storytelling) develops creative connections between subjects

**Communication & Collaboration:**
- Team-based storytelling fosters collaboration and empathy
- Multi-language support (11 languages) exposes children to different languages and cultures
- Voice recording develops oral language skills and articulation
- Narrative creation strengthens ability to organise and express ideas effectively
- Shared play encourages negotiation and celebrating each other's achievements
