The National Geographic Kids Junior Chemistry Lab Kit is a complete early learning chemistry set designed for young scientists ages 4 and up. ## Learning Outcomes (Expanded)

### Why Hands-On Learning Works

Research published in Chemistry Education Research and Practice (RSC Publishing, 2026) studying 237 third- and fourth-grade students demonstrated that combining storytelling with hands-on chemistry activities effectively promoted chemistry understanding. The study found:
- 85–98% of children successfully described observed phenomena across three experiment types
- Students progressed from intuitive to scientific model explanations
- Children's science perceptions are "constructed by age nine or ten, remaining unaltered in later schooling" — early intervention is critical to preventing science disengagement
- Word frequency analysis showed a measurable decrease in intuitive concepts and increase in scientific terminology after hands-on activities

The American Educational Research Association found that 62% of kindergarteners with limited exposure to science continue to struggle with science in elementary school — highlighting how much early exposure matters.

A study involving 250+ students in grades K–2 using Science4Us showed that students who received early, structured science engagement demonstrated significantly higher confidence in conducting science activities and substantially greater content mastery compared to control groups.

### Future-Ready Skills & Career Connections

Research from Purdue University's GEMS programme (Global Engineering and Manufacturing Supplement) confirms that students who receive early STEM exposure demonstrate:
- Enhanced problem-solving skills with improved critical thinking abilities
- Measurable advancements in mathematical reasoning
- Significantly higher self-efficacy in science subjects

Early chemistry exposure specifically builds:
- Laboratory literacy (how to use tools, follow procedures safely)
- Scientific vocabulary that supports later learning
- Comfort with the scientific method — the basis for all STEM careers

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics projects that STEM occupations will grow nearly 11% over the next decade (vs. 5% for all occupations). Starting children on the path to scientific confidence at age 4 gives them more than a decade of compounding advantage before they reach secondary school.
