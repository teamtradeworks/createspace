### Product Overview

The Makerzoid Robot Master Tournament (MKZ-RM-TRT) is a competition-grade STEM robotics kit specifically engineered to train children for competitive robotics tournaments. It sits above the standard Robot Master line and is positioned as the highest-tier kit for children who want to take their robotics skills into competitive environments. The kit introduces children to the fundamentals of programming, mechanics, and electronics through building, programming, and controlling physical robot models.

The Tournament edition is described as "designed to achieve high results in competitions for children aged 10 and over." While entry-level models in the Robot Master series start at age 6, the Tournament version is specifically aimed at older children who are ready for a more advanced challenge and competition preparation.

## Learning Outcomes (Expanded)

### Why Hands-On Learning Works

Research consistently shows that hands-on, project-based learning produces significantly better outcomes than passive instruction. Educational robotics is one of the most effective tools in this category:

- A 2024 meta-analysis published in the *International Journal of STEM Education* found that educational robotics has **positive effects on students' knowledge (effect size g = 0.528), skills (g = 0.600), and attitudes (g = 0.287)** — with a moderate overall effect of g = 0.57 on learning outcomes.

- A seven-year longitudinal study of 862 students (ages 7–10) published in PMC found:
  - **89% gain in coding skills** (from 3% coding competency pre-program to 92% post-program; p < 0.001)
  - **67% gain in robotics design knowledge** (from 26% to 93%; p < 0.01)
  - **32% increase in career motivation and interest** in computing careers (p < 0.05)
  - Extended participation showed cumulative benefits: correlation between program duration and coding gains (r = 0.269, p < 0.05)

- A 2024 Frontiers in Education systematic review found that approximately **83% of effect size estimates were positive** for educational robotics' impact on STEM competence in primary education.

- Physical robots "boost the three main categories of learner engagement and intrinsic motivation" — students are more engaged with tangible, programmable objects than screen-only learning.

- Research on Scratch programming specifically (the platform used in this kit) shows that it develops computational thinking including "algorithm dimension, critical thinking, collaborative thinking, and problem-solving ability."
