### Product Overview

The AI Vision Kit is a modular hardware expansion for the VinciBot coding robot that introduces children to the foundational concepts of artificial intelligence and computer vision. Rather than passive learning about AI, children interact directly with machine vision technology — programming their robot to see, recognise, and respond to the world around it.

The kit adds a 180° flip camera, a 1.5-inch full-colour programmable LCD screen, and dual fill lights to the VinciBot. It comes pre-loaded with seven AI vision functions covering face recognition, colour detection, line following, AprilTag tracking, and handwriting recognition. Beyond the pre-trained models, children can also train their own custom AI models through a one-click data collection and training process — experiencing the full machine learning pipeline hands-on.

The coding interface is graphical block-based, making it accessible without prior text-coding experience, while still connecting to Scratch and Python pathways as children progress.

## Learning Outcomes (Expanded)

### Why Hands-On AI Learning Works

Research increasingly supports that children learn AI and computational concepts most effectively when they can interact with and manipulate the technology directly, rather than passively studying it. A 2025 systematic review of 258 studies on AI in elementary STEM education (arXiv, 2025) found that when children take on the role of "teaching" AI systems — collecting data, training models, and evaluating outputs — they activate the same metacognitive processes documented in peer-teaching research, producing measurably stronger learning outcomes than passive instruction.

A separate meta-analysis of educational robotics across multiple studies found that robotics-based learning produces a **moderate-to-large effect on STEM learning performance (mean effect size 0.665)** and a moderate effect on learning attitudes (0.497). Children engaged with physical, programmable robots show significant improvements in computational thinking, problem-solving, and confidence in STEM subjects.

A 7-year study of 862 children aged 7–10 in a robotics and coding programme (PMC, 2024) found:
- **89% improvement** in coding proficiency (from 3% to 92%)
- **67% improvement** in robotics design understanding
- **32% increase** in computing career aspirations

The AI Vision Kit builds on this by letting children experience the full machine learning workflow — data collection, model training, deployment, and real-world testing — at a level accessible to ages 8–12.
