## Learning Outcomes (Expanded)

### Why Hands-On Learning Works
- **Building from instructions builds spatial skills.** The University of Surrey SPACE study (409 children, Surrey and Portsmouth schools) had children build models from pictorial instructions in twelve 30-minute sessions over six weeks. Compared with standard instruction, they showed larger gains in spatial and maths ability. The Surrey BLOCS summary notes that building from instructions engages the spatial skills that matter for maths more than free building does. Following a multi-step illustrated manual (as with this 175-piece build) is exactly this kind of task.
- **2D to 3D translation.** A 2025 Indiana STEM Education Conference brief (Purdue) found that K–6 students who designed and built models turned 2D plans into 3D structures and showed improved spatial visualisation.
- **Tinkering builds persistence.** NAEYC's *Making and Tinkering with STEM* describes tinkering as a playful starting point where curiosity drives learning, and lists resilience (persistence through trial and error) as a key outcome. Informal STEM programmes have been linked to increased persistence, interest and engagement in STEM practices (cited in a 2024 *Child Development* family-tinkering study).
- **Science at home builds confidence.** NC State research found that children's access to science items at home was linked to their self-confidence in science.
- **Seeing inside a "black box".** The clear casing makes an invisible process visible: kids watch the cause-and-effect chain (motor → crank → pistons → camshaft → valves) that is normally hidden under a car bonnet.

### STEM Skills

**Mechanical Engineering:**
- How rotary motion is converted to linear motion (and back) via crankshaft and connecting rods
- Gear trains and timing: how gears link the crankshaft to the valves so everything moves in sync
- How valves open and close on a schedule (cam/rocker action)
- How multiple cylinders fire in sequence to produce smooth power (firing order)

**Physics & Science of Combustion Engines:**
- The four-stroke cycle: intake, compression, power (combustion), exhaust
- Role of the spark plug in igniting the fuel-air mixture (represented by flashing LEDs)
- Energy conversion: chemical energy in fuel → heat → motion
- History of the combustion engine (Learning Guide)

**Electrical Basics:**
- Battery-powered motor drives the model
- Simple circuit for the LEDs and sound, switched by a push button

**Engineering Practice:**
- Reading technical, illustrated assembly diagrams
- Fine-motor precision with a screwdriver and small parts
- Sequencing a large build and checking work as you go
- Troubleshooting when something doesn't move as expected

### Four-Stroke Cycle (background for content)
1. **Intake:** the intake valve opens and the piston moves down, drawing in a fuel-air mixture.
2. **Compression:** both valves close and the piston moves up, squeezing the mixture.
3. **Power:** the spark plug fires, the mixture burns rapidly and pushes the piston down, turning the crankshaft.
4. **Exhaust:** the exhaust valve opens and the piston pushes the used gases out.
The cycle repeats continuously while the engine runs. In a 4-cylinder engine each cylinder is at a different stroke at any moment, so there is always one cylinder on its power stroke.
