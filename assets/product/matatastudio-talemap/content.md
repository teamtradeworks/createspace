### Manufacturer Information

**Source:** https://www.matatastudio.com/talemap.html | https://shop.matatastudio.com/

**Note on product naming:** The product is officially called **TaleMap** by MatataStudio. The Shopify handle `matatastudio-talemap` is a compressed version of this name. All external sources refer to it as "TaleMap" or "Tale Map."

### Product Overview

TaleMap is a 6×6 definable OID (Object Identification) interactive map system designed exclusively for use with the MatataStudio Tale-Bot Pro coding robot and MatataCode software. It transforms the Tale-Bot Pro experience from a guided robot activity into a fully open-ended storytelling and coding platform — children and teachers can create unlimited custom interactive maps and scenarios by inserting clip cards into the 36-pocket grid and programming the robot's responses using free PC software.

Unlike the Activity Box (a structured accessory with preset activities), TaleMap is a creative, teacher-led tool. There are no fixed activities — the 106 included clip cards covering mathematics, language, geography, animals, fruit and more serve as scene-building materials, and the TaleMap Library within MatataCode provides ready-made .imap files to download and explore.

### Specifications
- **Grid Format:** 6×6 definable OID interactive map with 36 card pockets
- **Clip Cards:** 106 double-sided clip cards
- **Card Themes:** Mathematics, language, geography, animals, fruit, and more
- **Dimensions:** 30 × 10 × 18 cm
- **Weight:** 0.7 kg
- **Compatibility:** Tale-Bot Pro only
- **Software Required:** MatataCode (TaleMap version) — Windows 64-bit, version 1.0.0, free download

### Features
- **Customisable OID Map:** 36 pockets on the 6×6 grid accept clip cards or everyday items (leaves, coins, etc.) to create unique story scenes
- **Unlimited Scenarios:** Any combination of the 106 clip cards creates a different map — no two sessions need to be alike
- **Design Your TaleMap Tool:** Core feature of MatataCode software; assigns an Action + Voice response to each of the 36 OID grid positions
- **TaleMap Library:** Pre-made .imap files within MatataCode ready for immediate download to Tale-Bot Pro — no custom design required
- **Story-Based Coding:** Combines movement, sound, and storytelling in a single hands-on activity
- **Teacher-Friendly:** Maps can be saved, shared, and reused across multiple sessions or classes
- **Screen-Free Play:** Once the .imap file is loaded to the robot via USB, no screen is needed for the child during play
- **Cross-Curricular:** Clip cards span mathematics, language, geography, science themes, and creative subjects
- **3D Possibility:** Can be used to build 3D background environments (similar to the MatataMap product line)

### What's Included
- 1× 6×6 Definable OID Interactive Map
- 106× Double-sided clip cards (covering mathematics, language, geography, animals, fruit, and more)

## Learning Outcomes (Expanded)

### Why Hands-On, Screen-Free Coding Works for Young Children

Research consistently supports tangible, physical coding as the most developmentally appropriate introduction to computational thinking for children aged 3–8.

A 2022 study by Weipeng Yang published in the *British Journal of Educational Technology* compared robot programming (using Matatalab/MatataStudio robots — the exact same product family as TaleMap) against traditional block play in kindergarteners. The robot programming group showed **greater improvements in sequencing ability** over time, with children who had lower initial self-regulation benefiting most. The study assessed six computational thinking categories: algorithms, modularity, control structures, representation, hardware/software, and debugging.

Research from Dr. Marina Bers' DevTech Research Group found that children using screen-free coding robots **performed 27% better** than their screen-based counterparts on computational thinking assessments covering sequencing, loops, conditionals, and debugging.

Educational theorist Seymour Papert described physical robots as "objects to think with" — when children program a robot, their code produces observable real-world results rather than abstract on-screen graphics. This is especially powerful for pre-readers and early learners.

### STEM Skills

**Computational Thinking:**
- Sequencing: planning the order of a robot's steps to navigate from A to B
- Algorithmic thinking: breaking a goal (e.g. "collect all the vegetables") into step-by-step instructions
- Debugging: identifying why the robot went the wrong way and correcting the commands
- Loops: writing repeating instructions to complete tasks efficiently

**Mathematics:**
- Spatial reasoning and directional thinking (forward, backward, left, right)
- Counting and number recognition through mathematics-themed clip card scenarios
- Pattern recognition across grid positions
- Measurement concepts through robot movement distance (each square = 10×10 cm)

**Language & Literacy:**
- Storytelling through physical scene-building with clip cards
- Narrative sequencing: beginning, middle, end of a robot's journey
- Vocabulary development through themed card sets (geography, animals, fruit, etc.)

**Science & Discovery:**
- Cause-and-effect understanding through programmed robot responses
- Observation and prediction skills
- Introduction to how technology responds to input commands

### Activity & Scenario Types (Open-Ended System)

TaleMap is a creative platform rather than a fixed-project kit. The 106 double-sided clip cards and the TaleMap Library together support a wide range of teacher- and child-designed scenarios.

**Clip Card Theme Categories (from manufacturer):**
- Mathematics (shapes, numbers, counting, patterns)
- Language (letters, words, storytelling elements)
- Geography (maps, places, environments)
- Animals (wildlife, habitats, life cycles)
- Fruit and food (categorisation, sorting activities)
- General themes (everyday objects and scenes)

**Types of scenarios children can create and play:**

| Type | Description | Coding Concepts Taught |
|------|-------------|------------------------|
| Navigation challenges | Code the robot to move from one card to another in a specific sequence | Sequencing, directional commands |
| Collection games | Guide the robot to "collect" specific themed items across the map | Loops, counting, sequencing |
| Storytelling journeys | Create a narrative scene (e.g. a farm or jungle) and code the robot to visit story points | Sequence, creativity, narrative thinking |
| Sorting activities | Arrange cards by theme, then navigate to matching pairs | Classification, pattern recognition |
| Custom adventure maps | Teachers or parents design a map around a lesson topic (e.g. life cycle of a butterfly) | Cross-curricular, algorithmic thinking |
| Pre-made library maps | Download ready-made .imap files from the TaleMap Library and explore immediately | All basic coding concepts |

**Difficulty Progression:** TaleMap does not have a fixed progression — scenarios can be made easier (shorter paths, fewer squares) or more complex (longer routes, conditions, loops) based on the child's level. The open-ended format means a 3-year-old and an 8-year-old can use the same product at very different complexity levels.

### Life Skills & Character Development

**Emotional Regulation:**

**Creativity & Innovation:**

- Children become creators rather than consumers — they design the world the robot lives in
- Storytelling merges creative writing and logical thinking simultaneously

**Collaboration:**

**Communication:**

## Additional Notes

### Quality & Safety
- Designed by MatataStudio (MATATALAB CO., LTD.), an education technology company founded in China with international distribution
- The clip cards are designed for young children — durable card stock construction
- OID technology is non-electronic (optical pattern recognition) — no electrical hazard in the map itself
- The product requires adult supervision for software setup (USB connection to computer)
- No sharp edges or small hazards specific to the map product; the robot (sold separately) should be checked for age-appropriate safety

### Longevity
- Virtually unlimited replayability — no two maps need to be the same
- The TaleMap Library in MatataCode is an ongoing resource (pre-made .imap files can be updated by manufacturer over time)
- As children grow (up to age 8), scenarios can be made progressively more complex
- Teachers can build up a bank of saved maps for repeated classroom use
- Compatible with the broader MatataStudio/MatataCode ecosystem (Tale-Bot Pro accessories, Activity Box, Exploration Maps)

### Setup Time
- Initial setup: ~10–15 minutes (install MatataCode software on Windows PC, create or download an .imap file, transfer via USB)
- Per-session setup: 2–5 minutes (insert clip cards, ensure robot is charged and loaded with .imap file)
- Child-facing play begins immediately once the map and robot are ready

### Unique Features
- **OID technology**: Each of the 36 map positions has an invisible optical code that the Tale-Bot Pro reads to trigger its programmed response — this is what makes the interactivity possible without any screen
- **Everyday item integration**: Parents and teachers can replace clip cards with real objects (leaves, coins, small toys) to create personalised, seasonal, or topic-specific scenes — this is a creative differentiator vs other coding map products
- **TaleMap Library**: Ready-to-use .imap file library removes the barrier to entry for teachers who are new to coding — no design skills required to get started
- **Cross-curricular by design**: Unlike many coding tools that are solely about programming, TaleMap explicitly bridges coding with literacy, maths, geography, and science through its themed clip cards
- **Reusability for schools**: Because the system is customisable rather than consumable, a single TaleMap kit can be used indefinitely across multiple classes and school years
