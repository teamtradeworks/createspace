- MatataStudio educator course, Activity Cards lesson: https://matatalab.com/en/lesson4.4
- Official single-robot page: https://shop.matatastudio.com/products/tale-bot-pro (meta description: "screen-free coding robot for ages 3–5")
- matatalab.com Tale-Bot Pro page: https://matatalab.com/en/tale-botpro (materials and safety claims)

**Confirmed:**
- **Age:** MatataStudio designs Tale-Bot Pro and the Activity Cards for ages 3–5 (lesson 4.4: "Applicable age: children aged 3~5"). The classroom set spec says "Age 3+". In South African terms that is roughly Grade 00 to Grade R, so the page frames the set as best for Grade R and Grade 1.
- **No app or screen:** Official FAQ: standard coding play uses only the robot's buttons. The MatataCode app is only used with the optional TaleMap system for designing custom maps. (The same page also says kids can "create their own songs and dance with MATATACODE App".)
- **No Scratch support.** Nothing on MatataStudio's Tale-Bot Pro pages, FAQ or educator course mentions Scratch. "Matatalab Scratch" on matatalab.com is only a software menu link for other products. Earlier claims below that Tale-Bot Pro supports Scratch were wrong and have been corrected.
- **Charging:** 2.5 h with a 5V 2A adapter. Service time ≥4 h. 1500mAh battery. ABS body "in line with ROHS".
- **Materials and safety (matatalab.com):** eco-friendly food-grade material, sturdy and fall-resistant, lead-free.
- **No teacher guide, lesson plans, assessment rubrics or progress tracking.** The teaching materials are the 42 Activity Cards, Challenge booklet, User guide and Callout stickers quick guide.
- **Activity Cards format (from the sample PDF):** each card has a code (A-1 to D-10), a cross-curricular tag (Coding/CS, Science, Math, Social Study, Storytelling, Social Emotional), a "Class Hours" estimate, a materials list, numbered illustrated steps and a "Bonus" box (an extension idea or a teacher discussion prompt). The A cards also list key vocabulary.
  - Sample cards are marked **3+ (A, B and D cards) or 4+ (all three C cards)**. Class hours are **1 or 2** (B-3 and D-2 are 2).
  - Some cards need everyday materials (A-2 uses 10 paper cups).
  - Back cover: coding concepts "Commands, Sequence, Loops"; cross-curricular "Math, Social Emotional, Social Studies, Science, Art".
- **Lesson length and pacing (lesson 4.4):** 20–30 minutes per class, 15–20 classes per semester. Activities are designed for **pairs** ("A group of 2 people works together"). "Teacher: No CS teaching/programming experience required."
- **Educator course:** free, self-guided "Course 4: Matatalab Tale-Bot Pro Course" (https://matatalab.com/en/node/808), six lessons (4.1 to 4.6) ending in a final quiz; educators get a certificate when they finish it. Lesson pages are readable without logging in.
- **In the box (official image):** Tale-Bot Pro x6, Accessories x18, Washable markers x12, USB-C cable x6, Paper crafts x2, Challenge booklet x1, Sticker booklet x3, Configuration card x1, User guide x1, Command cards x6, Activity cards x1 (42 activities), Callout stickers x3, Callout stickers quick guide x1, Double-sided blank map x3, 5 double-sided interactive maps (10 themes) x3. That is 18 maps: 15 interactive and 3 blank. The 10-port charging station is in the official meta description; the charging station and storage bin are not drawn in the box image.
- **Map themes (from the box image and sample cards):** My Five Senses, Counting Game, Shape Monster, Sunflower life cycle, Frog life cycle, My Town, Zoo Visit, Hardworking Bee, plus two more whose labels aren't legible in the image.
- **Dance:** 6 dance moves are preset and there is a Random Dance button. Card C-2 "Tireless Dancer" has learners design dance steps with the arrow buttons and repeat them with the Repeat button.
- **LEGO:** "Compatible with large LEGO® bricks" for building characters and scenes. No Activity Card category covers LEGO construction or engineering; LEGO bricks appear only as an optional bonus on A-2.

**Not confirmed:**
- **Language count.** Neither official page states how many languages the robot speaks. Retailers disagree (RobotShop says 10, Stemfinity says 11). The configuration card in the official box image shows six flags (US, France, Germany, Russia, Italy, Spain). Afrikaans and other South African languages are not mentioned anywhere. Don't publish a language count until MatataStudio confirms it.

## Learning Outcomes (Expanded)

### Why Screen-Free Group Learning Works

Research consistently shows that screen-free, tangible coding robots are more effective than screen-based tools for developing computational thinking in Foundation Phase learners. A study published in the British Journal of Educational Technology found that robot programming groups experienced greater gains in sequencing ability compared to block play control groups. A landmark study on coding in primary grades found that improvements in planning and inhibition skills after just 1 month of coding activities were equivalent to or greater than improvements attained after 7 months of standard educational activities — supporting the value of early computational thinking education.

Group-based learning with physical robots is particularly effective because:
- Peer teaching reinforces understanding — when one learner explains to another, both benefit
- Observable cause-and-effect (press button → robot moves) makes programming tangible and memorable
- Physical interaction activates motor memory, reinforcing learning patterns
- Cooperative structures build communication and social-emotional skills alongside coding

The PRIMM framework (Predict, Run, Investigate, Modify, Make) is fully supported by Tale-Bot Pro's design: learners predict what will happen, run their code, investigate the result, modify their approach, and make something new. This structured cycle of scientific thinking is foundational to computational thinking education.

Research with comparable screen-free robots shows that the screen-free group performed 27% better than screen-based groups on tests of computational thinking including sequencing, understanding of loops and conditionals, and debugging.
