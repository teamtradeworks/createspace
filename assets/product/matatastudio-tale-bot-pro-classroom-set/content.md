## Learning Outcomes (Expanded)

### Why Screen-Free Group Learning Works

Research consistently shows that screen-free, tangible coding robots are more effective than screen-based tools for developing computational thinking in Foundation Phase learners. A study published in the British Journal of Educational Technology found that robot programming groups experienced greater gains in sequencing ability compared to block play control groups. A landmark study on coding in primary grades found that improvements in planning and inhibition skills after just 1 month of coding activities were equivalent to or greater than improvements attained after 7 months of standard educational activities — supporting the value of early computational thinking education.

Group-based learning with physical robots is particularly effective because:
- Peer teaching reinforces understanding — when one learner explains to another, both benefit
- Observable cause-and-effect (press button → robot moves) makes programming tangible and memorable
- Physical interaction activates motor memory, reinforcing learning patterns
- Cooperative structures build communication and social-emotional skills alongside coding

The PRIMM framework (Predict, Run, Investigate, Modify, Make) is fully supported by Tale-Bot Pro's design: learners predict what will happen, run their code, investigate the result, modify their approach, and make something new. This structured cycle of scientific thinking is foundational to computational thinking education.

Research with comparable screen-free robots shows that the screen-free group performed 27% better than screen-based groups on tests of computational thinking including sequencing, understanding of loops and conditionals, and debugging.

### Complete Activity Cards Inventory (42 Activities)

The Activity Cards are the structured curriculum at the heart of the Classroom Set — distinct from the Challenge Booklet included in the individual Tale-Bot Pro. They are specifically designed for educator-led classroom implementation.

**Category A — Basic Instructions (10 activities)**
Purpose: Help learners get familiar with Tale-Bot Pro and master basic command buttons
Concepts: Directional commands (forward, backward, left, right), button operation, cause and effect, robot response

**Category B — Exploration (15 activities)**
Purpose: Expand children's thinking through health, science, social, and language themes; introduce sequence
Concepts: Sequencing, cross-curricular integration (science life cycles, social scenarios, language activities, health topics), narrative coding

**Category C — Pattern Recognition (7 activities)**
Purpose: Help learners identify patterns and learn programming repeat (loops) in scene contexts
Concepts: Loops, pattern recognition, repeated sequences, programming efficiency

**Category D — Overall Development (10 activities)**
Purpose: Comprehensive activities combining all previously learned programming knowledge in more complex themed scenes
Concepts: Commands + sequences + loops combined, problem-solving, creative application, open-ended exploration

**Note:** Individual activity titles are not publicly listed — the categorisation framework above is what is documented by MatataStudio.

**Activity Design Principles:**
- Scene-based learning with coherent game/story contexts for each activity
- Designed for cooperative pair or small group work (2–4 learners per robot)
- Each activity cross-references: ELA (language expression), Mathematics, Science, Art, or Social Studies
- Activities inspire educators to create additional activities beyond the 42 provided
- Difficulty progression: A → B → C → D from foundational to comprehensive
