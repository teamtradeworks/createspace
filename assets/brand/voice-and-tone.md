# CREATESPACE Voice & Tone Guide

A quick-reference rubric for writing and auditing all CREATESPACE content — website, product pages, education pages, and marketing materials.

For full brand strategy, see [brand-strategy.md](./brand-strategy.md).

---

## Voice Summary

**We are:** Fun, playful, enthusiastic, trustworthy, knowledgeable, inviting, integral.

**We are not:** Elitist, complicated, nerdy/stereotypical, dry/boring, gimmicky, corporate, childish.

**In practice, this means:** We sound like a knowledgeable friend who's genuinely excited about STEM — not a corporate brochure, not a hype machine, and not a lecturer. We're specific, honest, and approachable.

---

## The Golden Rules

1. **Be specific, not sweeping.** "Ages 8–12" not "kids everywhere." "15 guided projects" not "endless possibilities."
2. **Be honest, not inflated.** We're a small South African STEM store. We don't need to sound like a global movement.
3. **Show, don't proclaim.** Describe what the product does. Don't announce how important or revolutionary it is.
4. **Earn every word.** If a sentence could be deleted without losing information, delete it.
5. **Write for the reader, not ourselves.** Parents want to know "is this right for my child?" Educators want to know "does this work in my classroom?" Answer those questions directly.

## Words & Phrases to Avoid

These patterns consistently push our content away from our brand voice. Flag and rewrite whenever they appear.

### Grandiose language

| Avoid | Why |
|-------|-----|
| "inspire the next generation" | Overused, sounds like a corporate mission statement |
| "revolutionise / revolutionize" | We sell STEM kits, not revolutions |
| "groundbreaking" | Almost never literally true |
| "cutting-edge / state-of-the-art" | Vague superlatives that add no information |
| "world-class" | Unverifiable, sounds corporate |
| "pioneering" | Grandiose — save for actual firsts |
| "transform learning / transform playtime" | Overclaims what a product can do |
| "ignite a passion" | Fine in our internal vision statement, too much for customer-facing copy |
| "game-changer" | Cliche (exception: direct customer quotes) |

### Corporate filler

| Avoid | Why |
|-------|-----|
| "empower" (as marketing verb) | Corporate buzzword. Say what we actually enable instead |
| "passionate group united by a common goal" | Generic corporate "about us" language |
| "unlock potential / unlock possibilities" | Vague and overused |
| "your gateway to..." | Sounds like a sales brochure |
| "journey" (as in "coding journey") | Overused to the point of meaninglessness |
| "making a meaningful difference" | Vague feel-good filler |
| "fuel their curiosity" | Acceptable in brand strategy, too abstract for product pages |

### Overclaiming

| Avoid | Why |
|-------|-----|
| "every teaching need" / "every child" / "everything you need" | No product satisfies every need. Be specific about what it *does* cover |
| "give your child a head start" | Cliche, implies other children are falling behind |
| "future-proof" | Nobody can guarantee the future |
| "all you need to..." | Usually not literally true |
| "proven to..." | Requires actual proof. If we have it, cite it. If not, don't claim it |

### Unverifiable social proof

| Avoid | Why |
|-------|-----|
| "Join thousands of families" | We can't verify this number — and it sounds like every other e-commerce site |
| "Join 8,000+ / 20,000+ schools worldwide" | These are manufacturer stats, not ours. Irrelevant to a South African store |
| "Trusted by schools across South Africa" | Only use if we can back it up with names or numbers we actually know |
| "Join hundreds of schools" | Same problem — sounds inflated |

## Sounding Human (Not AI-Generated)

Most of our copy is drafted with AI help. AI writing has specific tells that make it read as generic and untrustworthy, which is the opposite of our voice. As a final pass on any drafted copy, run it through the **humanizer** skill (`.agents/skills/humanizer/SKILL.md`), or check it against these patterns by hand.

Watch for and rewrite:

- **Em dashes and en dashes.** Cut them. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead. This is the single most reliable AI tell.
- **Rule of three.** AI forces ideas into groups of three ("innovation, inspiration, and insight"). Break the pattern; say the one or two things that actually matter.
- **Promotional filler.** "vibrant", "testament", "boasts", "nestled", "rich", "seamless", "stands as". This overlaps with the grandiose-language table above.
- **Superficial "-ing" clauses.** Sentences padded with "...highlighting its importance", "...ensuring a great experience", "...fostering creativity". Delete or replace with a concrete claim.
- **Negative parallelisms.** "It's not just a kit, it's an adventure." Say the real thing plainly.
- **Fake profundity.** "At its core...", "The real question is...", aphorisms like "X is the language of Y". Replace with the ordinary point underneath.
- **Signposting.** "Let's dive in", "Here's what you need to know." Just say the thing.
- **Title Case Headings and stray emojis.** Use sentence case; keep emojis out unless there is a real reason.

Humanizing refines *how* copy reads. It must never change a factual claim, and it must preserve everything in the rules above (specificity, honesty, no invented social proof).

## Claims & Social Proof Rules

1. **Only claim what we can verify.** We're a small South African business. We don't have thousands of customers — and we don't need to pretend we do.
2. **No invented statistics.** Round-number social proof ("Join 8,000+ schools") from manufacturers is not our data. Don't present it as though it is.
3. **Testimonials must be real.** Use named customers with real quotes. Attribute properly. Don't paraphrase testimonials into marketing speak.
4. **Stats need a source.** If we cite a statistic (e.g. "75% of future jobs will require STEM skills"), we must be able to point to where it came from. If we can't, remove it.
5. **Be proportionate.** Our social proof is strong enough without inflation. A real quote from one teacher at a local school is more credible than "trusted by thousands."
6. **Manufacturer stats stay with the manufacturer.** "MatataStudio is used in 20,000+ schools worldwide" is a statement about MatataStudio, not about us. If relevant, attribute it clearly. Don't fold it into our own CTAs.

## Heading Guidelines

### Structure
- Every page has exactly **one `<h1>`** (provided by the HeroSection component or the page itself — never both).
- Use `<h2>` for major sections, `<h3>` for subsections within them.
- Never skip levels (no `<h1>` → `<h3>`).

### Writing headings
- **Be specific and descriptive.** A heading should tell the reader what the section is about, even out of context.
- **Apply the title test:** Could this heading apply to any STEM kit? If yes, rewrite it to be product-specific.
- **Short and functional is fine.** "What's in the Box", "Key Features", "How It Works" — these are clear and helpful.
- **Questions work well** when they match what the reader is actually wondering: "Does my child need experience?" is better than "Getting Started."

| Good headings | Bad headings |
|---------------|-------------|
| "93+ Projects from Beginner to Expert" | "Packed with Possibilities" |
| "A Pocket-Sized Programmable Computer" | "From Imagination to Reality" |
| "What Families Are Building" | "Real Families, Real Learning" |
| "CAPS-Aligned Robotics Activities" | "Curriculum Ready" |
| "What's in the Box" | "Included Components" |

### Headings to avoid
- **Vague:** "In Action", "Get Started", "Learn More", "Choose Your Path"
- **Overclaiming:** "Everything You Need", "The Complete Solution", "Resources for Every Teaching Need"
- **Cliche:** "The Future Starts Here", "Where Learning Meets Fun"

## CTA Guidelines

### Title
- Direct and action-oriented: "Get Started", "Browse the Range", "Equip Your Classroom"
- Do not repeat the hero tagline in the CTA title
- Avoid emotional manipulation or false urgency

### Subtitle
- Should add new information or reinforce a concrete selling point
- Not a place for grandiose claims or unverifiable social proof
- Good: "Free delivery on orders over R750" / "170-page project guide included"
- Bad: "Join thousands of families who trust CREATESPACE to inspire the next generation"

### Buttons
- Primary: "Add to Cart" (links to `#product-actions`)
- Secondary: "Browse More Kits" (links to `/shop` or relevant collection)
- Keep button text short (2–4 words)

## Content by Page Type

### Product pages (parent audience)
- See [product-content-framework.md](./product-content-framework.md)
- Tone: fun, enthusiastic, reassuring
- Address: "your child", parents' concerns about age/skill/independence
- Accuracy: cross-reference all specs, project counts, and features against `assets/product/{slug}/content.md`

### Education pages (educator audience)
- See [edu-product-content-framework.md](./edu-product-content-framework.md)
- Tone: professional-enthusiastic, knowledgeable, trustworthy
- Address: "your learners", educators' concerns about curriculum/cost/support
- Use SA education terminology: "learners" not "students", reference CAPS, use grade ranges

### Core pages (mixed audience)
- Homepage, about, contact — speak to both parents and educators
- Be welcoming and clear about what we do
- Don't try to sound bigger or more important than we are
- Let our products and customer testimonials do the convincing

## South African English

- Use South African spelling: colour, favourite, programme, organise, specialise
- Use "delivery" not "shipping"
- Use "VAT" not "tax"
- Use "learners" not "students" in education contexts
- Use "Rand" / "R" for currency: R1,200 (comma for thousands, period for decimals)
- Reference CAPS curriculum, not other countries' standards

## Before & After Examples

### Hero tagline
- **Before:** "Inspire the next generation of scientists, engineers, and creators."
- **After:** "Curated STEM kits for curious kids — from first circuits to advanced coding."

### CTA subtitle
- **Before:** "Join thousands of families and schools who trust CREATESPACE to inspire the next generation of innovators."
- **After:** "Free delivery on orders over R750. 30-day returns on all products."

### CTA subtitle (product page)
- **Before:** "Join 8,000+ schools worldwide using MatataStudio to teach computational thinking through screen-free play."
- **After:** "Screen-free coding for ages 4–9. No batteries or devices needed."

### CTA title
- **Before:** "Give Your Child a Head Start in Coding"
- **After:** "Get Started with Coding"

### About page intro
- **Before:** "We're a passionate group of educators, engineers, and STEM enthusiasts united by a common goal: making science and technology accessible and exciting for every child in South Africa."
- **After:** "We're a team of educators and engineers who believe STEM should be hands-on, fun, and available to every kid in South Africa."

### Education page CTA
- **Before:** "Join hundreds of schools across South Africa who are already inspiring the next generation of innovators."
- **After:** "Get in touch to find the right STEM programme for your school."

### Section heading
- **Before:** "Resources for Every Teaching Need"
- **After:** "STEM Programmes for Schools"

### Section heading (product page)
- **Before:** "In Action"
- **After:** "See It in the Classroom" (or better: describe what's shown — "Learners Building Robots")

### Product description
- **Before:** "Designed by robotics professors and engineers, Makerzoid transforms playtime into powerful learning moments."
- **After:** "Designed by robotics professors and engineers, Makerzoid kits teach real mechanical engineering through building and play."

### Social proof
- **Before:** "Join 20,000+ schools and institutions worldwide using MatataStudio to teach the next generation of AI innovators."
- **After:** "MatataStudio is used in over 20,000 schools worldwide." (attributed as a manufacturer fact, not our CTA)

### Education page description
- **Before:** "Empower your educators to teach STEM with confidence."
- **After:** "Give your teachers the tools and training to teach STEM with confidence."
